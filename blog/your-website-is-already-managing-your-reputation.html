Your website is already managing your reputation. The question is how well.

Reviews and testimonials on your own site do more work than most people give them credit for.

BS
Brent ScioneMay 15, 2026

A website's reputation section does more work than most businesses give it credit for. It's one of the only places a visitor can look at real customer feedback without leaving the site, which matters, because most people check reviews before they buy or sign up regardless of how good the rest of the page looks.

Testimonials placed directly on a site work differently than reviews on a third-party platform. They're curated, sure, but they're also right next to the offer, at the exact moment someone's deciding whether to trust it. A dedicated testimonials page, or a few well-placed quotes near a pricing section, reassures a visitor at precisely the point they're hesitating.

Structured data like review, product, and organization schema helps search engines and AI systems connect trust signals to your business, and for eligible pages can surface rich details directly in search results. Making trust proof clear in both human copy and structured markup gives prospective buyers confidence before they ever fill out a form.

Actually collecting reviews takes intention. A simple, direct link to Google Reviews or another platform, sent at the right moment (right after a good interaction, not three weeks later in a generic newsletter) gets meaningfully more responses than hoping someone thinks to leave one unprompted. Responding to reviews, especially the negative ones, does more for credibility than people expect too. A thoughtful, professional response to a bad review often reads better to a future customer than a page of nothing but five stars, because it shows there's an actual person behind the business who takes feedback seriously.

Beyond reviews, case studies, awards and media mentions round out the picture. All of it is social proof, and all of it belongs somewhere on the site instead of scattered across platforms a visitor has to go hunting for. A business's reputation is being formed online whether anyone's actively managing it or not. The website is simply the one place that reputation can actually be shaped instead of just observed.
